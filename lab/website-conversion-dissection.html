A website doesn't have a conversion problem. It has a reason.

The GOAT's five-system framework for finding where a website loses qualified buyers.

1. Intent alignment

Does the page answer the problem that brought the visitor there? Search query, ad promise, landing-page headline and offer should feel like one continuous conversation.

2. Message clarity

A visitor should quickly understand what you do, who it is for, why it matters and what to do next. Clever copy cannot compensate for ambiguity.

3. Trust

Proof should sit near the decision it supports. Relevant work, process transparency, real credentials and clear business information reduce uncertainty better than generic claims.

4. Friction

Every unnecessary field, competing CTA, slow interaction, confusing navigation path or unanswered objection adds resistance. The goal is not “fewer elements”; it is fewer unnecessary decisions.

5. Measurement

A form submission is not revenue. Track the path from source and landing page through qualified lead, appointment, sale and revenue whenever the business systems allow it.

GOAT NOTE: Optimize the weakest link first. Increasing traffic into a broken conversion path usually just buys more evidence that the path is broken.

WORKED DISSECTION · ILLUSTRATIVE, NOT A CLIENT RESULT

Start with a specific failure

Imagine an emergency-repair ad landing on a homepage that lists every service. The visitor must find repair coverage, opening hours and the contact button. First align the landing-page headline with the repair request, state the real service area and hours, and put the relevant contact action beside those details.

Write a testable hypothesis

“Visitors who need repairs will find the correct inquiry path more easily when it appears beside coverage and hours.” Measure accepted repair inquiries per eligible visit, then check lead quality and response capacity. Do not declare a winner from a handful of clicks.

Record what changed

Document the page version, traffic source, date range and qualification rules. Hold other major changes steady where possible and inspect phone and form outcomes together. If the result is inconclusive, keep the uncertainty in the report.

Example A: unclear offer

“We handle everything.” A generic service list and three competing buttons leave the visitor to find the repair inquiry path.

Example B: focused next step

“Need a repair?” The page states the actual coverage and opening hours, explains the next step, and offers one clear repair-request action.
